Question. What prime gaps are hidden in the continued fraction of the square root of two?

Make the question precise

The continued fraction expansion of √2 is [1; 2, 2, 2, ...]. This repeating sequence suggests a pattern in the sequence of prime numbers. Specifically, we are interested in the gaps between consecutive primes in this sequence of numerators.

Work an example, a counterexample, or a special case

To illustrate, consider the first few numerators of √2's continued fraction: 1, 3, 7, 17, 41, 99, 239, 577, ... These are the prime numbers 2, 3, 7, 17, 41, 99, 239, 577, ... Notice the gaps: 2, 4, 10, 24, 80, 218, 540, 1352, ... These gaps are not immediately obvious from the sequence of numerators alone, but they do seem to follow a pattern.

For instance, the gap between 7 and 17 is 10, which is 2 * 5, and the gap between 17 and 41 is 24, which is 2 * 12. This suggests a potential sieve-like mechanism at play, though the exact nature remains a mystery.

What would count as an answer

A full answer to this question would require a proof of a conjecture about the distribution of prime gaps in the continued fraction of √2. This could involve deep number theory, including properties of continued fractions, Diophantine approximations, and perhaps even connections to other areas of mathematics like analytic number theory or algebraic geometry.

Left on the chalkboard
