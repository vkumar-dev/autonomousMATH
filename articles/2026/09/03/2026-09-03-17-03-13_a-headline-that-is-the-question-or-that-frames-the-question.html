A martingale with a twist on concentration fails to hold

What happens when we apply a concentration inequality to a martingale that's not quite a martingale?

Question. Can a martingale satisfy a concentration inequality if the inequality requires the martingale to be nonnegative?

Make the question precise

In the foundational side of Real Analysis, a martingale is a sequence of random variables \( X_0, X_1, X_2, \ldots \) such that \( \mathbb{E}[X_{n+1} \mid X_0, X_1, \ldots, X_n] = X_n \) for all \( n \). A concentration inequality provides a bound on the deviation of \( X_n \) from its mean.

Consider a martingale \( M_n \) with values in \( \mathbb{R} \). Can \( M_n \) satisfy the concentration inequality \( \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq t) \leq \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq 0) \) for all \( t \)?

Work an example, a counterexample, or a special case

Let's explore this with a simple example. Consider a martingale \( M_n \) defined by \( M_n = X_n - \mathbb{E}[X_n \mid X_0, X_1, \ldots, X_{n-1}] \), where \( X_n \) are independent and identically distributed (i.i.d.) random variables with \( \mathbb{E}[X_n] = 0 \) and \( \text{Var}(X_n) = 1 \).

For this martingale \( M_n \), \( \mathbb{E}[M_n] = 0 \) and \( \text{Var}(M_n) = n \). The concentration inequality \( \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq t) \leq \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq 0) \) fails because \( M_n \) can be negative for \( n > 0 \).

What would count as an answer

The martingale \( M_n \) fails to satisfy the concentration inequality \( \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq t) \leq \mathbb{P}(M_n \geq 0) \) for all \( t \). This counterexample demonstrates that a concentration inequality for a martingale requires the martingale to be nonnegative.

Left on the chalkboard

The foundational side of Real Analysis is robust, but the failure of a concentration inequality to hold for a nonnegative martingale challenges our understanding of how random variables can be controlled.
