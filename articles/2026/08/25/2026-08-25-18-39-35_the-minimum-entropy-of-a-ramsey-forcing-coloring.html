The Minimum Entropy of a Ramsey-Forcing Coloring

Why does finding a structure cost information? A toy model of the informational price of Ramsey's theorem.

Question. For a fixed integer $t \ge 2$, let $\mathcal{C}_n$ be the set of all 2-colorings of the edges of the complete graph $K_n$. What is the minimum Shannon entropy $H(\mathcal{D}) = -\sum_{c \in \mathcal{C}_n} \mathcal{D}(c) \log \mathcal{D}(c)$ of a probability distribution $\mathcal{D}$ on $\mathcal{C}_n$ such that $\mathcal{D}$-almost every coloring contains a monochromatic $K_t$?

Why do structures have entropy?

We are in a tension between two disciplines. Ramsey Theory says that if you color enough edges, structure pops out. Information Theory says that structure costs bits. If I force you to hand me a monochromatic $K_t$, I am paying for the bits you used to create it. But can we quantify this payment? Let $\mathcal{C}_n$ be the set of all functions $c: E(K_n) \to \{0, 1\}$. The size is $2^{\binom{n}{2}}$. A deterministic coloring has entropy 0. But a deterministic coloring often fails to be Ramsey. To guarantee a monochromatic $K_t$, $\mathcal{D}$ must be a "heavy-tailed" distribution over the combinatorial space of partitions. The question is not about the worst-case coloring. It is about the average-case distribution. If $\mathcal{D}$ is the uniform distribution, $H(\mathcal{D}) = \binom{n}{2}$. If $\mathcal{D}$ concentrates on the Ramsey witness, $H(\mathcal{D})$ drops. We want the drop.

Let's break the axiom of determinism

Suppose we drop the axiom that the witness must be a single object. Suppose the "channel" is a random variable $X: \{0, 1\}^{\binom{n}{2}} \to \{0, 1\}$, the output of which is the color of the "major" edge. We require $\mathbb{P}[X \text{ is monochromatic on } K_t] = 1$. Consider $t=3$. We need a triangle. In a standard Ramsey proof, we fix a vertex, look at its neighbors, and use the Pigeonhole Principle. That principle is the axiom of "deterministic dominance." If we replace it with "random dominance," we are asking: How much entropy must the distribution have to ensure that the Pigeonhole Principle holds for almost all draws? Example: $n=5$, $t=3$. A specific monochromatic $K_3$ has entropy 0. But the distribution must cover *all* $K_5$'s. The minimal entropy is achieved by a "minimal covering" distribution. It is not the uniform distribution. It is a distribution that places mass on the fewest number of colorings that form a "Ramsey cover" of $K_5$. The calculation is not trivial. It involves the asymptotic expansion of the Ramsey number $R(K_t, K_t)$ and the combinatorial entropy of the graph partitions.

The minimum entropy for $K_4$ is related to the entropy of the Ramsey graph itself, which is $\Theta(n^2)$, but the constant hidden in the $\Theta$ is the answer we are trying to extract.

What counts as the answer?

We are not asking for the Ramsey number. We are asking for the *entropy of the witness*. If the answer is $\Theta(n^2)$, then "structure is free" in the sense that it costs the same bits as the graph itself. If the answer is $\Theta(n)$, then structure is "cheap"—you only need a linear number of bits to force a triangle. The "break" comes if the answer is $O(n \log n)$. That would mean that a tiny amount of randomness forces a triangle. But we know that $R(K_3, K_3) = 9$, which is constant. If the entropy of the distribution is $O(1)$, then the distribution is trivially Ramsey. But for large $n$, the graph is not Ramsey. So the entropy must grow. The question is: Does it grow like the number of edges ($n^2$), or does it grow like the number of vertices ($n$), or does it grow like the number of triangles ($n^2$) but with a hidden constant?

Left on the chalkboard

We have posed the question. The answer lies in the interplay between the combinatorial structure of the graph and the probabilistic measure on the space of colorings. If we can construct a "low-entropy Ramsey channel," we have found that structure is cheaper than we thought. If not, then the cost of structure is fundamental to the combinatorial universe.

Take-home question: What is the entropy of the minimal Ramsey covering of $K_n$?
